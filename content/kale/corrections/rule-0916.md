title: "@deva[अर्ह्] with Infinitive for Request or Ability"
pages: [516.png]
verdict: approved
---
The root @deva[अर्ह्] 'to deserve' when used (in the second person) in combination with the infinitive expresses 'a request, a respectful entreaty,' and is generally, equivalent to the English 'I pray, be pleased, &c.;' @deva[अग्निं शमयितुमर्हसि] (Meg. 55) please put out the fire; @deva[न चेद्रहस्यं प्रतिवक्तुमर्हसि] (Kum V. 40) if you have nothing to conceal from me, please answer me: @deva[द्वित्राण्यहान्यर्हसि सोढुमर्हन्] (Rag. V. 25) pray wait for two or three days, O respectable one! &c. Sometimes it is equivalent to a gentle command: @deva[इमां प्रसादयितुमर्हसि] (Rag. I. 89) you ought to please her; @deva[न तं शोचितुमर्हसि] you ought not to bewail him. In the third person and under the same circumstances it expresses power or ability and can be translated by 'can:' @deva[द्रोणं हि समरे कोऽन्यो योद्धुमर्हति फाल्गुनाद्] Mah. Bhar. IV. 58. 27. @deva[दैवं प्रज्ञाविशेषेण को निवर्तितुमर्हति] Ibid I. 1. 246.

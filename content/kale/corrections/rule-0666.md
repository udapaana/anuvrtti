## Unādi Affixes and Introduction to Participles

There is a peculiar class of Kṛt or Primary affixes technically designated by Sanskrit grammarians—*Unādi* or those beginning with the affix @deva[उण्] *i. e.* the affix @deva[उ] with the mute or indicatory letter @deva[ण्], so called from the words @deva[कारु], @deva[वारु], &c. in the first Sûtra being derived with the affix. These Unādi affixes form primary nouns, like other Kṛt affixes form verbal roots, but are classed separately because their application is limited, because the nouns derived by their means are either formed irregularly, or the connection between their senses and the meanings of roots from which they are supposed to be derived is not so clearly discernible as in the case of other primary derivatives; *e. g.* @deva[अश्नुते अध्वानं व्याप्नोतीति वा अश्वः] 'a horse' which is derived either from @deva[अश्] 'to pervade,' or from @deva[अध्वन्] 'road;' and @deva[आप्] with @deva[वि], &c.; @deva[कारु] 'an artisan' from @deva[कृ] 'to do,' &c.

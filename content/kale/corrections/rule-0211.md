**(a)** It is not compounded with verbal derivatives[^1] ending in @deva[तृ] and @deva[अक] and denoting the agent of the action; @deva[अपां स्रष्टा] and not @deva[अप्स्रष्टा]; @deva[घटस्य कर्ता], @deva[ओदनस्य पाचकः] &c.; but @deva[इक्षूणां भक्षणम् इक्षुभक्षिका] as the @deva[अक] here does not denote the agent.

**Exception**[^2]:—But a compound is allowed in the case of the words @deva[याजक] one who employs as sacrificing priests, @deva[पूजक], @deva[परिचारक], @deva[परिवेषक] a waiter at meals, @deva[स्नापक] a servant who bathes his master or brings bathing water for him, @deva[अध्यापक], @deva[उत्सादक] one who destroys, @deva[होतृ], @deva[भर्तृ], when it does not mean 'a holder,' and others not very important: @deva[ब्राह्मणयाजकः], @deva[देवपूजकः], @deva[राजपरिचारकः] &c.; @deva[अग्निहोता], @deva[भूमर्ता], &c. but @deva[वज्रस्य भर्ता] 'the thunder-bolt-bearer'.

**(b)**[^3] A specific Genitive (i. e. from which a singling out is made) is not compounded with any other words, as @deva[नृणां द्विजः श्रेष्ठः]

**(c)**[^4] A noun in the Genitive is not compounded with ordinal numbers, words denoting qualities, words implying satisfaction, present participles, past participles meaning 'honoured or approved of' or showing the place of the act, verbal indeclinables and verbal nouns ending in @deva[तव्य]; e. g. @deva[सतां षष्ठः], @deva[ब्राह्मणस्य शुक्लाः] (@deva[दन्ताः]); @deva[काकस्य कार्ष्ण्यम्]; @deva[फलानां सुहितः] 'satisfied with fruits' (an Inst. Tat. is, of course, allowed in these cases); @deva[द्विजस्य कुर्वन् कुर्वाणो वा किंकरः], @deva[सतां मतः] esteemed by the good; @deva[राज्ञां पूजितः]; @deva[इदमेषां आसितं] (place of sitting) @deva[गतं भुक्तं वा]; @deva[ब्राह्मणस्य कृत्वा], @deva[नरस्य कर्तव्यम्] &c.

**N. B.** Compounds like @deva[राजपूजितः], @deva[राजमतः], &c. ought to be considered as Instrumental Tatpurushas.

**Exceptions.**

**(1)** If the word denoting a quality ends in @deva[तर] it does combine with a noun in the Genitive, and the @deva[तर] is dropped; @deva[सर्वेषां श्वेततरः सर्वश्वेतः] whiter than all others; similarly @deva[सर्वेषां महत्तरः सर्वमहान्] &c.

**(2)**[^5] The words @deva[द्वितीय], @deva[तृतीय], @deva[चतुर्थ] and @deva[तुर्य] are compounded with nouns signifying the whole of which they are parts, and are optionally placed first, @deva[द्वितीयं भिक्षायाः द्वितीयभिक्षा], @deva[भिक्षाद्वितीयम्] half of the quantity of alms; but @deva[द्वितीयं भिक्षायाः] (begging alms a second time) @deva[भिक्षुकस्य].

**Note.**—Compounds like @deva[द्वितीयभिक्षा], @deva[पूर्वकायः] (see further on) &c. are not strictly Gen. Tat. as the case of the first word determines the name, but mere Tat.; some call these Nom. Tat. (@deva[प्रथामतत्पुरुष]).

**(a)**[^6] A noun in the Genitive, used as an object in a sentence, when the agent and object of the action denoted by the nouns formed by kṛit affixes are both used, is not compounded; as @deva[आश्चर्यो गवां दोहोऽगोपेन] 'the milking of cows by one, not a cowherd, is a wonder.'

## 6. UPAPADA COMPOUNDS.

When the second member of a Tat. Comp. is a root-noun derived from a root which takes a particular Kṛit affix by virtue of the presence of the first member, it is called an Upapada Tat.; as @deva[कुम्भं करोतीति कुम्भकारः] 'a potter;' so @deva[साम गायतीति सामगः] 'one who chants a verse of the Sāman Veda;' @deva[मांसं कामयती मांसकामा] 'desirous of meat'; similarly @deva[अश्वक्रीती] (@deva[अश्वेन क्रीता]) 'bought for a horse', @deva[कच्छपी] 'a female tortoise,' &c. The noun prefixed to the last member, such as @deva[कुम्भ], is called an *upapada*.[^1]

**N. B.** The latter member of such compounds must neither be the conjugated form of a root nor a separate verbal derivative i. e. formed independently of the last member; e. g. @deva[पयोधर] is not an Upapada Tat., as the word @deva[धर] can be independently formed; it is a Gen. Tat. @deva[धरतीति धरः पयसां धरः पयोधरः] 'a cloud' or 'the female breast.'

---

[^1]: @deva[तत्रोपपदं सप्तमीस्थम् ।] Pāṇ. III. 1. 92. @deva[सप्तम्यन्ते पदे कर्मणीत्यादौ वाच्यत्वेन स्थितं कुम्भादि तद्वाचकं पदमुपपदसंज्ञं स्यात् ।] Sid. Kau.

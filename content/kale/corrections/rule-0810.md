## Passive Construction with Causative Verbs

When the causal forms are used in the passive,[^1] the principal object (i. e. the primitive subject) is put in the Nominative case; but in the case of roots implying 'knowledge or eating' and roots having a literary work for their object the principal object is put in the Nominative case and the secondary object in the Accusative or *vice versa*; @deva[देवदत्तः कटं करोति] Devadatta prepares a mat—@deva[देवदत्तं देवदत्तेन वा कटं कारयति]; @deva[देवदत्तः कटं कार्यते] he makes Devadatta prepare a mat, @deva[देवदत्तः ग्रामं गच्छति] Dev. goes to a village; @deva[देवदत्तं ग्रामं गमयति] causes Dev. to go to a village; @deva[देवदत्तः ग्रामं गम्यते]; Devadatta is made to go, &c.; @deva[माणवकं धर्मं बोधयति] he makes Māṇavaka know his duty; @deva[बोध्यते माणवकं धर्मः, माणवको धर्मं इति वा] Māṇavaka is made to know, &c.; @deva[बटुमोदनं भोजयति] he makes the child eat food; @deva[बटुरोदनं भोज्यते] or @deva[बटुमोदनो भोज्यते] the boy is made, &c.

---

[^1]: @deva[बुद्धिभक्षार्थयोः शब्दकर्मणां च निजेच्छया । प्रयोज्यकर्मण्यन्येषां ण्यन्तानां लादयो मताः ॥] Sid. Kau. on Pāṇ. VII. 1. 69.

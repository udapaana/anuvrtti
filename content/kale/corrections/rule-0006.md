In addition to the characters given above there are in Sanskrit two nasal sounds:—the one called Anusvāra, is denoted by @deva[ं] i.e. a dot placed above the letter after which it is to be pronounced, e.g. @deva[कं]; the other, called Anunāsika is denoted by @deva[ँ] i.e. a dot within a semi-circle placed above the letter after which it is to be pronounced e.g. @deva[सँ].

(a) And a sort of hard breathing is known as Visarga (generally called Visarjanīya by Sanskrit grammarians). It is denoted by the sign @deva[ः] i.e. two vertical dots placed after the letter after which it is to be pronounced. In pronunciation it is a harder aspirate than @deva[ह]. The Visarga is not an original character but only a substitute for a final @deva[स्] @[s] or @deva[र्] @[r].

(b) Jihvāmūlīya (@deva[जिह्वामूलीय]) and Upadhmānīya (@deva[उपध्मानीय]) are terms given to a sort of Semi-Visarga, when pronounced before @deva[क्], @deva[ख्], and @deva[प्], @deva[फ्] respectively. It is written symbolically as @deva[ᳵ]. These may be regarded as the spirants of @deva[कवर्ग] and @deva[पवर्ग] respectively.

When several words of the same form ( or dissimilar in form but of the same import[^1] ) are compounded together, only one of them is retained with the necessary number—e. g. @deva[रामश्च रामश्च रामौ], @deva[रामश्च रामश्च रामश्च रामाः ।] This is called @deva[एकशेषद्वन्द्वः]. In the case of the mas. and fem. genders the one of the mas. gender is retained;[^2] as @deva[हंसी च हंसश्च हंसौ]; similarly @deva[ब्राह्मणौ], @deva[शूद्रौ], @deva[अजौ], &c.

**(a)** This principle is further extended to some words dissimilar in form, as in the following examples:—@deva[भ्राता च स्वसा च भ्रातरौ]; @deva[पुत्रश्च दुहिता च पुत्रौ];[^3] @deva[माता च पिता च पितरौ] (see also @ref[193]), @deva[श्वश्रूश्च श्वशुरश्च श्वशुरौ][^4] (also @deva[श्वश्रूश्वशुरौ]); @deva[स च सा च तौ], @deva[स च देवदत्तश्च तौ], @deva[स च यश्च यौ] or @deva[तौ].[^5] In the case of words of the mas. fem and neu. gender that of the neu. gender is retained; as @deva[तच्च देवदत्तश्च ते]; @deva[तच्च देवदत्तश्च यज्ञदत्ता च तानि ।]

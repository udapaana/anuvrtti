## Causative Verbs: Subject in Accusative Case

In the case of the causals of roots implying 'motion,' 'knowledge,' 'eating,' roots having some literary work for their object, intransitive roots and the roots @deva[दृश्] and @deva[जल्प्], @deva[भाष्] with @deva[आ]; @deva[लप्] with @deva[वि], @deva[ग्रह्] and @deva[श्रु], their subject in the primitive sense is put in the Accusative case.[^1]

@deva[शत्रूनगमयत्स्वर्गं वेदार्थं स्वानवेदयत् ।]
@deva[आशयच्चामृतं देवान्वेदमध्यापयद्विधिम् ।]
@deva[आसयत्सलिले पृथ्वीं यः स मे श्रीहरिर्गतिः ॥] Sid. Kau.

That venerable Hari is my refuge who despatched the enemies (of gods) to the next world, explained the meaning of the Vedas to his (followers), made the gods drink nectar, taught Veda to the Creator and seated the earth (made it rest) on waters.

@deva[दर्शयति हरिं भक्तान्] he makes the devotees see Hari; @deva[जल्पयति भाषयति विलापयति धर्मं पुत्रं देवदत्तः; पुत्रं विद्यामग्राहयत्] (cf. Kum. I. 52); @deva[अश्रावयत्परिषदान्कथाम्]. But when the double causal is used the subject ceases to be the subject of the root in the primitive sense and therefore takes the Instrumental; @deva[गमयति देवदत्तः यज्ञदत्तं], @deva[गमयति देवदत्तेन यज्ञदत्तं विष्णुमित्रः]

*Obs*:—@deva[दृश्] is sometimes found used with the Dative; @deva[प्रत्यभिज्ञानरत्नं च रामायादर्शयन्कृती] Rag. XII. 54.

(a) The causals of @deva[नी], and @deva[वह्] when it has for its subject a noun other than one denoting a driver, govern the Instrumental of the primitive subject;[^2] @deva[नाययति वाहयति वा भारं भृत्येन] he makes his servant carry a load; but @deva[वाहयति रथं वाहान् सूतः] the charioteer makes the horses draw the chariot.

(1) The same holds good in the case of the causal of @deva[अद्] and @deva[खाद्];[^3] @deva[आदयति खादयति वा अन्नं बटुना] he makes the boy eat his food.

(2) @deva[भक्ष्][^4] when it has not the sense of @deva[हिंसा] 'pain or injury to a sentient thing' does the same; @deva[भक्षयत्यन्नं बटुना]; but @deva[भक्षयति बलीवर्दान् सस्यम्] (when the loss of corn causes pain to the mind).

(b) @deva[स्मृ] and @deva[ज्ञा] which denote a kind of knowledge are construed with the Instrumental, though the former when meaning 'to remember with pain' is sometimes found used with the Accusative also; @deva[स्मारयति ज्ञापयति वा देवदत्तेन]; @deva[अयि चंद्रगुप्तदोषा अतिक्रान्तपार्थिवगुणान् स्मारयन्ति प्रकृतीः ।] Vide also Śis. VI. 56.

(c) The causal of the denominative @deva[शब्दाय][^5] also is construed with the Instrumental; @deva[शब्दाययति देवदत्तेन] he causes Devadatta to make a sound.

Note:—[^6] By intransitive roots are meant such roots as are not capable of governing an object other than one denoting space, time, &c. and not such as, though transitive, may be used without their object being actually expressed; @deva[मासं आसयति देवदत्तं]; but @deva[देवदत्तेन पाचयति] and not @deva[देवदत्तं] though the object of @deva[पच्] is not actually expressed.

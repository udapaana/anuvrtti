## PERFECT.

[^1]The Perfect denotes an action done before the current day and not witnessed by the speaker. It has reference to a very remote time and should, therefore, be used in narrating events of the remote past; @deva[तां ताटकाख्यां निजघान रामः।] Rāma killed her whose name was Tāṭakā. @deva[प्रययाविन्द्रजित्प्रत्यक्] (Bhaṭṭi. XIV. 16.).

(a) In the 1st person the Perfect shows that the speaker was in a distracted state of mind or was unconscious when the event took place, or that he wants utterly to deny something that he has done, @deva[बहु जगद पुरस्तात्तस्य मत्ताङ्किलाहम्] (S'is. XI. 39) being frenzied I prattled much, I am told, before him; @deva[कलिङ्गेष्ववात्सीः किं।] didst thou dwell in the country of the Kalingas? @deva[नाहं कलिङ्गाञ्जगाम] I never went to Kalingas. With these exceptions the Perfect should not be used in the 1st person.

## Eight Gaṇas (Syllabic Feet)

In the case of metres regulated by syllables each line is divided into groups of three syllables each, called Gaṇas or syllabic feet, named as @deva[म], @deva[न], @deva[भ], @deva[य], @deva[ज], @deva[र], @deva[स] and @deva[त]. The names and the Schemes of these are given in the following stanza.

@deva[मस्त्रिगुरुस्त्रिलघुश्च नकारो भादिगुरुः पुनरादिलघुर्यः ।]
@deva[जो गुरुमध्यगतो रलमध्यः सोन्तगुरुः कथितोन्तलघुस्तः ॥]

i. e. @deva[म] has all its syllables long; @deva[न] has all its syllables short; @deva[भ] has its first syllable long, @deva[य] has its first syllable short; @deva[ज] has its middle syllable long; @deva[र] has its middle syllable short; @deva[स] has its last syllable long and @deva[त] has its last syllable short.[^1]

The symbol ⏑ stands for a short or light syllable; the symbol – denotes a long vowel or a heavy syllable. Symbolically represented these Gaṇas will stand as under—

@deva[म] – – –  @deva[ज] ⏑ – ⏑
@deva[न] ⏑ ⏑ ⏑  @deva[र] – ⏑ –
@deva[भ] – ⏑ ⏑  @deva[स] ⏑ ⏑ –
@deva[य] ⏑ – –  @deva[त] – – ⏑

Similarly the letter @deva[ल] is used to denote a short syllable and @deva[ग] a long one at the close of a line.

[^1]: The following verse is easier to remember than one given above; @deva[आदिमध्यावसानेषु यरता यान्ति लाघवम् । भजसा गौरवं यान्ति मनौ तु गुरुलाघवम्]

## Instrumental: Use, Need, Accompaniment, Possession

The following words also govern the Instrumental case:—

(a) Words expressing the idea of use or need, such as @deva[किं], @deva[कार्यं], @deva[अर्थः], @deva[प्रयोजनं] and the like, and the root @deva[कृ] with @deva[किं] used in the same sense; @deva[धनेन किं यः] &c what is the use of his wealth who &c; @deva[तृणेन कार्यं भवतीश्वराणाम्]—'even the rich sometimes stand in need of grass,' similarly @deva[कोर्थः पुत्रेण जातेन यो न विद्वान्न धार्मिकः], @deva[न स्वामिपादानां मया किमपि प्रयोजनम्], &c.

(b) The particles @deva[अलं] and @deva[कृतं] meaning 'enough,' @deva[अलं रुदितेन] 'enough of your weeping (do not weep):' @deva[कृतं अत्यादरेण] 'away with overpressing'; @deva[अलं] is also used with a gerund @deva[अलं अन्यथा संभाव्य] 'away with misunderstanding.'

(c) Particles expressing 'accompaniment' such as @deva[साकं], @deva[सार्धं], @deva[समं], @deva[सह], &c.; @deva[आस्व साकं मया सौधे] (Bh. VIII. 70.); @deva[वनं मया सार्धमसि प्रपन्नः] Rag. XIV. 63; @deva[आहो निवत्स्यति समं हरिणाङ्गनाभिः ।] Ś'ak I. 27. &c.

(d) Words meaning 'having or destitute of,' @deva[समायुक्तोप्यर्थैः] (@deva[परिभवपदं याति कृपणः]) 'though possessed of wealth,' &c; @deva[अर्थेन हीनः] 'destitute of wealth.'

*N. B.* For the optional use of the Instrumental see the Abl., the Gen. and the Loc. cases.

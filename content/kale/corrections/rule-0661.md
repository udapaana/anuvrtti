**CHAPTER XIII.**

*Parasmaipada and Atmanepada.*

## Parasmaipada and Ātmanepada: Meaning of the Two Padas

As already remarked there are two Padas in Sanskrit, the Parasmaipada and the Atmanepada. The Parasm. denotes that the fruit of the action accrues to some one different from the agent, as @deva[पचति] 'he cooks for another,' @deva[कारयति] 'makes some one do something for another, &c.' The Atm. denotes that the fruit of the action is @deva[कर्तृगामि] *i. e.* it accrues to the agent, @deva[पचते] 'he cooks for himself,' @deva[कारयते] 'he makes another do something for himself,'[^1] &c.

(a) If, however, there be a word showing the accrual of the fruit of the action to the agent, the Atm. is optionally used, as @deva[स्वं यज्ञं यजते] or @deva[यजति] 'he offers his own sacrifice,' @deva[स्वं कटं कुरुते] or @deva[करोति] 'he weaves his own mat,' @deva[स्वं यज्ञं कारयति-ते] &c.

(b) When the causal form of a transitive verb is used reflexively, or when the object in the primitive sense becomes the agent in the causal, the Atm. is used except in the sense of 'remembering with eagerness, &c.;' @deva[भक्ताः भवं पश्यन्ति] 'devotees see Bhava;' @deva[भवो भक्तान् दर्शयते] 'Bhava shows himself to his devotees,' but @deva[स्मरति वनगुल्मं कोकिला]; @deva[स्मरयति वनगुल्मः (कोकिलं)]; @deva[उत्कण्ठापूर्वकस्मृतौ विषयो भवतीत्यर्थः] (Sid. Kau.). Vide Sid. Kau. on Pān. 1. 3. 67.

(c) When the agent of the action denoted by a verb cannot be other than an animal, the causal of the verb takes the Par. even when the action refers to the agent as @deva[कृष्णः शेते] 'Krishna sleeps,' @deva[गोपी कृष्णं शाययति] 'the cowherdess lulls Krishna to sleep,' but @deva[फलं पतति] 'the fruit falls down.' @deva[वायुः फलं पातयति] 'the wind causes the fruit to fall down,' &c.

(d) In the case of verbs meaning 'to eat,' except @deva[अद्], and 'to move,' their causals take the Parasm. even when the fruit of the action refers to the agent; @deva[निगारयति], @deva[आशयति] 'makes another eat,' @deva[चालयति], @deva[कम्पयति], 'he shakes' &c.

Exceptions to (c) and (d)—to (c) @deva[दम्] 'to pacify,' @deva[यम्] with @deva[आ] 'to draw in' @deva[यस्] with @deva[आ] 'to endeavour,' @deva[परिमुह्] 'to faint,' @deva[रुच्] 'to shine,' @deva[वद्] 'to speak,' @deva[वस्] 'to dwell', and @deva[घे] 'to drink;' to (d)—@deva[पा] 'to drink,' @deva[नृत्] 'to dance;' in the case of these roots the usual rule holds good; @deva[दमयति], @deva[दमयते]; @deva[शमयति-ते] &c.

[^1]: This distinction, however, seems to be very little observed in practice. Even the best Sanskrit writers are found using both the Padas promiscuously. It cannot even be supposed that this distinction is meant to be observed where a root admits of both the Padas. The Dashakumārácharita and the Kádambari afford several instances in which the two Padas are used exactly in the same sense.

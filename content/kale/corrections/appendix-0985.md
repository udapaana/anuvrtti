## Section I. Samavṛttas

*Metres with 8 Syllables to a Pāda.*

### (1) @deva[अनुष्टुभ्] or @deva[श्लोक].

This is the commonest of all Sanskrt metres; it forms the chief metre of the great epics and many of the Purāṇas, There are many Varieties of this metre; but that in common use has eight syllables in a Pāda the fifth being short (though occasional variations from these rules occur in the Mahābhārata and the Rāmāyaṇa):—

Ex.:—Vide Ist canto of Rag.

### (3) @deva[उपजाति.]

Def. @deva[अनन्तरोदीरितलक्ष्मभाजौ पादौ यदीयावुपजातयस्ताः ।]
@deva[इत्थं किलान्यास्वपि मिश्रितासु वदंति जातिष्विदमेव नाम ॥]

Sch. of G.—A mixture of @deva[इन्द्रवज्रा] and @deva[उपेन्द्रवज्रा] gives rise to the metre called @deva[उपजाति]. It is said to have fourteen different varisties. For examples see Rag. II. &c, Kum. III. Kir. XVII. Bhaṭṭi. II. &c.

When other two metres are mixed in one stanza, the mixture is still called Upajāti, as in the following verse from the S'isupālavadha which is a combination of @deva[वंशस्थविल] and @deva[इन्द्रवंशा]—

@deva[इत्थं रथाश्वेभनिषादिनां प्रगे गजो नृपाणामथ तोरणाद्बहिः ।]
@deva[प्रस्थानकालक्षमवेशकल्पनाकृतक्षणक्षेपमुदैक्षताच्युतम् ॥]

### @deva[दण्डक]

Metres with 27 syllabes or more in each *pāda* are designated by the general name *Daṇḍaka*. Many varieties of this are mentioned (as the number of syllables in each *pāda* may sometimes reach 999), such as @deva[चंडवृष्टिप्रयात], @deva[प्रचितक], @deva[मत्तमातंगलीलाकर], @deva[सिंहविक्रान्त], @deva[कुसुमस्तवक], @deva[अनंगशेखर], @deva[संग्राम] &c.; Māl. Mād. V. 23. is an instance of the species last named, having 54 letters to a *pāda*.

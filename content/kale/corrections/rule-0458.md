(a) Roots of more than one syllable, derived roots and roots of the 10th class are always Seṭ.

(b) Of all monosyllabic roots ending in a vowel those given in the following couplet (Kārikā) are Seṭ and the rest Aniṭ.

[^1]: The following couplet gives the endings of these roots and their number in each:— @deva[कचछजा दधनपा नमशाः षसहाः क्रमात्] @deva[।] […: the second line of the couplet, which gives the numbers in small figures, cannot be read with certainty on the scan.] The first line gives the ending consonants and thus enables the students to know at once which root is Seṭ and which is Aniṭ; e. g. @deva[कुट्] may be at once known to be a Seṭ root as @deva[ट्] does not occur in the first line. The second line gives the number of roots ending in a particular consonant. Thus roots ending in @deva[क] are @deva[क] i. e. one (@deva[क] being the first consonant) in number; roots ending in @deva[च] are @deva[च] i. e. 6, (@deva[च] being the 6th consonant); roots ending in @deva[छ] are @deva[क] i. e. one, in number, and so on.

[^2]: These couplets as well as those bearing on the 2nd variety of the Aorist are composed by Mr. Chintámana Átmárám Kelkar, the present learned S'astri at the Poona Training College.

As already remarked (See @ref[54]) there are three numbers in Sanskrit: a singular number, denoting a single individual, a dual number denoting two, and a plural number denoting more than two. Besides these general senses—

(a) The singular may be used to denote a class; @deva[सिंहः श्वापदराजः] the lion is the king of beasts; @deva[बुद्धिमत्सु नरः श्रेष्ठः] &c.

(b) The dual sometimes denotes a male and a female of the same class; @deva[पितरौ] parents; @deva[चटकौ] a male and a female of sparrow.

(1) Note:—Words like @deva[द्वय], @deva[द्वितीय], @deva[युग], @deva[द्वंद्व], &c. meaning 'a pair,' &c which are dual in sense but singular in form ought to be always used in the singular, except when several pairs are meant.

(2) Note:—Words like @deva[हस्तौ], @deva[नेत्रे], @deva[पादौ], &c. should always be used in the dual in Sanskrit.

(c) The plural, like the singular, may represent a class, @deva[ब्राह्मणाः पूज्याः] (or @deva[ब्राह्मणः पूज्यः]) Brāhmaṇas (i. e. the Brāhmaṇa class) are adorable.

(1) The plural is not unfrequently used as a mark of respect or reverence. @deva[इति श्रीशंकराचार्याः]; so (says) the venerable Shankarāchārya; @deva[इति आचार्यपादाः] this is the opinion of the revered preceptor, &c.

(2) In the first person, great personages and writers sometimes use the plural instead of the singular; @deva[वयमपि भवत्यौ किमपि पृच्छामः] we (i. e. I,) too ask you something; @deva[इति तु वयं] but we (i. e. I, the writer) hold this opinion; @deva[वयमपि च गिरामीश्महे] we rule over speech (i. e. language).

(3) Words like @deva[दाराः], @deva[गृहाः], @deva[अक्षताः], @deva[सिकताः], @deva[आपः], @deva[प्राणाः], @deva[लाजाः], &c. are always used in the plural, though some of them are singular in sense.

(4) Names of countries which are really the names of the people inhabiting them, must be used in the plural; @deva[स विदेहान् उपाययौ] he went to Videha, &c.

But in the case of compounds ending in words denoting a country, such as @deva[देश], @deva[विषय], &c. the sing. must be used; @deva[अस्ति मगधदेशे पाटलिपुत्रं नाम नगरम्] there is a town called Pāṭaliputra in the country of Magadha.

(5) The plural of proper nouns denotes a family or race as in English; @deva[जनकानां रघूणां च यत्कृत्स्नं गोत्रमङ्गलम्] Utta.

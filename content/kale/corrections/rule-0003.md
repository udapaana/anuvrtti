The nine primary vowels consist of five simple vowels viz. @deva[अ], @deva[इ], @deva[उ], @deva[ऋ] and @deva[ऌ], and four diphthongs viz. @deva[ए], @deva[ऐ], @deva[ओ] and @deva[औ].

(a) A vowel may be @deva[ह्रस्व] or short, @deva[दीर्घ] or long, and @deva[प्लुत] or protracted (also called prolated by some) according to the time required to pronounce it.[^1] The vowels are thus divided into—

(1) Short vowels—@deva[अ] @[a], @deva[इ] @[i], @deva[उ] @[u], @deva[ऋ] @[ṛ], @deva[ऌ] @[ḷ];

(2) Long vowels—@deva[आ] @[ā], @deva[ई] @[ī], @deva[ऊ] @[ū], @deva[ॠ] @[ṝ], @deva[ए] @[e], @deva[ऐ] @[ai], @deva[ओ] @[o], @deva[औ] @[au]; and

(3) Protracted vowels @deva[आ३] @[ā3], @deva[ई३] @[ī3], @deva[ऊ३] @[ū3], @deva[ॠ३] @[ṝ3], @deva[ऌ३] @[ḷ3], @deva[ए३] @[e3], @deva[ऐ३] @[ai3], @deva[ओ३] @[o3], and @deva[औ३] @[au3].

**N.B.** As the @deva[प्लुत] or protracted vowels are not commonly to be met with in classical Sanskrit, the vowels are usually given as thirteen, represented by the thirteen signs given above under (1) and (2).

(b) Each of these vowels may be again of two kinds, @deva[अनुनासिक] or nasalised, and @deva[अननुनासिक] or without a nasal sound.[^2]

(c) Vowels are also further discriminated into @deva[उदात्त] or acute, @deva[अनुदात्त] or grave, and @deva[स्वरित] or circumflex. @deva[उदात्त] is that which proceeds from the upper part of the vocal organs, @deva[अनुदात्त] that which proceeds from their lower part, while @deva[स्वरित] arises out of a mixture of these two.[^3] But these are ignored in classical Sanskrit. They are marked only in Vedic works: the Udātta is left unmarked; the Anudātta is marked with a horizontal line underneath; and Svarita has a perpendicular stroke above it. e. g. @deva[क्व१ वोऽश्वाः], &c. Ṛg. V. 61. 2 @deva[रथानां न पे२गाः ।] &c. Ṛg. X. 78. 4. @deva[शतचक्रं यो३ ह्यः] &c. Ṛg. X. 145. 4.

Thus there are eighteen different modifications of each of the vowels @deva[अ], @deva[इ], @deva[उ], @deva[ऋ], and twelve of @deva[ऌ], @deva[ए], @deva[ऐ], @deva[ओ] and @deva[औ]; for there is no long ḷ and the last four have not their corresponding short vowels.

[^1]: @deva[ऊकालोऽज्झ्रस्वदीर्घप्लुतः ।] Pāṇ. I. 2. 27. The crowing of the cock in the morning represents in its three stages these three kinds of vowels. The time required to pronounce a short vowel is called a @deva[मात्रा]. A long vowel has two mātrās and a Pluta vowel three.

[^2]: @deva[मुखनासिकावचनोऽनुनासिकः] Pāṇ. I. 1. 8.

[^3]: @deva[उच्चैरुदात्तः। नीचैरनुदात्तः। समाहारः स्वरितः।] — Pāṇ. I. 2. 29, 31.

## Genitive with @deva[हेतु] (Purpose, Cause)

[^1] When the word @deva[हेतु] (cause, object) is used in a sentence, that which is the object and the word @deva[हेतु] are put in the Genitive case; @deva[अन्नस्य हेतोर्वसति] dwells for the sake of (with the object of getting) food; @deva[रोदिषि कस्य हेतोः] Mark P. 23. 12. @deva[हेतोर्बोधस्य मैथिल्याः प्रास्तावीद्रामसंकथाम्] (Bhatti. VIII. 103.) he began to give her an account of Rāma in order to show her that he (Hanumat) was Rāma's messenger.

(a) [^2] When a pronoun is used with the word @deva[हेतु], the Instrumental and the Genitive may both be used; @deva[कस्य हेतोः], @deva[केन हेतुना], with what object? Why? The Ablative may also be used; @deva[तेन हेतुना], @deva[तस्माद्धेतोः], @deva[तस्य हेतो] ; when a word having the same sense as @deva[हेतु], such as @deva[निमित्त], @deva[कारण], &c, is used with a pronoun it may be used in any case in agreement with the pronoun; @deva[कस्य निमित्तस्य], @deva[कस्य प्रयोजनस्य]; @deva[केन निमित्तेन], @deva[कस्मै निमित्ताय] &c.; but they are generally used in the Accusative used like an adverb; @deva[किं-निमित्तं-कारणं-प्रयोजनं-अर्थं], &c. when a pronoun is not used, any case except the Nominative and the Accusative may be used; @deva[ज्ञानेन निमित्तेन] (@deva[हरिः सेव्यः]), @deva[ज्ञानाय निमित्ताय] with the object of acquiring knowledge.

---

[^1]: @deva[षष्ठी हेतुप्रयोगे ।] Pāṇ II. 3. 27.
[^2]: @deva[सर्वनाम्नस्तृतीया च । निमित्तपर्यायप्रयोगे सर्वासां प्रायदर्शनम् ।] Vārt.

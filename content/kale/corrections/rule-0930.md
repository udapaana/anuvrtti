## AORIST.

[^1]The Aorist simply expresses past action indefinitely i. e. without reference to any particular time (@deva[भूतसामान्ये लुङ्]); @deva[सोध्यैष्ट वेदांस्त्रिदशानयष्ट पितॄनपारीत्समममंस्त बन्धून्। व्यजेष्ट षड्वर्गमरंस्त नीतौ समूलघातं न्यवधीदरींश्च] (Bhaṭṭi. I. 2.) 'He studied the Vedas, offered sacrifices to the gods, satisfied his departed ancestors, honoured his relatives, subdued the collection of six (i. e. the six passions), took delight in politics and totally annihilated his enemies. The Aorist, however, properly denotes a recent action or one done during the course of the present day. "It is similar to the English present Perfect," remarks Dr. Bhandārkar[^2], "which the student will remember defines an action as having happened in a portion of time which is not yet expired; it brings past action in connection with the present time," @deva[अभूद्वृष्टिरद्य] it rained to-day.

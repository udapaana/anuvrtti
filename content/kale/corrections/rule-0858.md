## Exceptions: When Genitive is NOT Used

[^1] The Genitive is not used with present participles except that of @deva[द्विष्], verbal derivatives ending in @deva[उ] and @deva[उक] except that derived from @deva[कम्], verbal indeclinables, past participles, passive and active, nouns formed with the affix @deva[खल्] (see p. 456) and with such as mean 'in the habit of, or having the properties of or doing any thing well'; @deva[कर्म कुर्वन्] or @deva[कुर्वाणः]; but @deva[मुरं मुरस्य वा द्विषन् हरिः] Hari, the enemy of Mura; @deva[हरिं दिदृक्षुः] desirous of seeing Hari; @deva[हरिं अलङ्करिष्णुः], @deva[दैत्यान् घातुको हरिः] Hari is the killer of demons; @deva[लक्ष्म्याः कामुकः], @deva[जगत्स्रष्टा], @deva[सुखं कर्तुं] &c. @deva[विष्णुना हता दैत्याः], @deva[दैत्यान् हतवान्]; @deva[ईषत्करः प्रपञ्चो हरिणा] worldly life goes easy with Hari; @deva[आत्मानं अलङ्करिष्णुः] in habit of decorating oneself; @deva[अन्नं भिक्षुः] a habitual beggar; @deva[कर्ता कटं] one who prepares a mat; [^2] also in the case of nouns derived by means of the terminations @deva[अक] showing futurity and @deva[इन्] showing necessary payment; @deva[हरिं दर्शको याति] he goes desiring to see Hari; @deva[शतं दायी] one who has to pay a hundred (rupees.).

---

[^1]: @deva[न लोकाव्ययनिष्ठाखलर्थतृनाम् ।] Pāṇ. II. 3. 69. @deva[कमेरनिषेधः] Vārt.
[^2]: @deva[अकेनोर्भविष्यदाधमर्ण्ययोः ।] Pāṇ. II. 3. 70.

## I. PREPOSITIONS

## Definition and Function of Prepositions (Upasarga)

A preposition, styled 'Upasarga or Gati,' in Sanskrit is an indeclinable word, having an independent meaning and prefixed to verbs and also to their derivatives. These prepositions modify, intensify and sometimes totally alter the senses of roots[^1]; e. g. @deva[प्रहृ] 'to strike,' @deva[आहृ] 'to eat, to perform as a sacrifice,' @deva[संहृ] 'to contract,' @deva[विहृ] 'to sport,' @deva[परिहृ] 'to avoid, &c.' Sometimes they are prefixed without any alteration in the sense.

[^1]: @deva[धात्वर्थं बाधते कश्चित्कश्चित्तमनुवर्तते । तमेव विशिनष्ट्यन्य उपसर्गगतिस्त्रिधा ॥] Cf. also Sid. Kau. @deva[उपसर्गेण धात्वर्थो बलादन्यत्र नीयते । प्रहाराहारसंहारविहारपरिहारवत् ॥] Some think that prepositions have no meaning of their own, but they simply bring to light when prefixed to roots their hidden senses. (cf. Śiś. X. 15).

## Ablative for Cause, Argument, Comparison

(a) The Ablative often denotes the cause of an action or condition and may be translated by 'on account of, by reason of,' &c; @deva[मौनान्मूर्खः गण्यते] one is considered a fool by reason of his silence (if he keeps silence); @deva[गोमानुषाणां वधात्] (H.) on account of my killing cows and men.

(b) The Ablative is used to make a causative assertion or to advance an argument; @deva[पर्वतो वह्निमान् धूमात् ।] The mountain is fiery (has fire on it). because there is smoke; @deva[स्मृत्यनवकाशदोषप्रसङ्ग इति चेन्नान्यस्मृत्यनवकाशदोषप्रसंगात्] (Ved. Su. II 1. 1) A disputant says—if you say that our argument is liable to the fault of giving no scope to your smritis then we reply: this your argument will not hold; for in that case other smritis will have no scope given to them.

(c) The Ablative is used with comparatives or words having a comparative sense; @deva[भक्तिमार्गात् ज्ञानमार्गः श्रेयान्] the way of knowledge is more efficient than that of devotion; @deva[अणोरप्यणीयान्] smaller than an atom; @deva[अश्वमेधसहस्रेभ्यः सत्यमेवातिरिच्यते] truth alone is superior to a thousand horse-sacrifices; @deva[चैत्ररथादनूने] not inferior to Chaitraratha.
